Basic argument: domestic political institutions may be largely shaped by international politics and systems. Expands previous theories focusing primarily as IR as a consequence of domestic politics, rather than a cause

``Instead of being a cause of international politics, domestic structuremay be a consequenceof it. Internationalsystems, too, become causes insteadof consequences'' (Gourevitch, 1978, p. 882)

Two primary forces of IR on domestic politics: distribution of power among states (war), and distribution of economic activity (trade)

Dahl 1972 discusses conquest as a cause of internal structure, which is basically the ``simple'' theory that Gourevitch is expanding on.

``The very configurationwhich made it possible for Germanyand Japan, and to a lesser extent Italy, to catch up to Britainso rapidly(the survivalof classes, institutions, and values from a preindustrial,anticonstitutionalistera), also made those countriesmorevulnerableto fascism.'' (Gourevitch, 1978, p. 886)
