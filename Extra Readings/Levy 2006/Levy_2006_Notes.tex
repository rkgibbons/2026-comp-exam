\section{General Notes}
%--------------------------------------------------

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=4pt]
    \item From preface: the role of the state has drastically changed in advanced democracies, but the presence of the state (and all it entails) is still very much there. How did these goals change, and what does the state look like in this modern construction?
    \item Two main conclusions from questioning new state missions across advanced democracies:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item ``Contemporary developments in the economy, technology, society, and politics are not just challenging existing state activities but also creating demands and opportunities for new state intervention.''
        \item ``The spread of a liberal, market logic has been an especially powerful prod to state activism. In short, the move to the market has been, at the same time, a move to and through the state.''
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Shonfield: modern capitalism as ``the changing balance of public and private power,'' characterized by growth of public intervention in five areas (all quotes):
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item Management of the economic system
        \item Keynesian demand stimulus, the welfare state, and expanded public expenditures
        \item The ``taming'' of the ``violence of the market'' in the private sector, thanks to government regulation, planning, and inter-firm cooperation
        \item Policies to increase innovation and worker training
        \item The pursuit of intellectual coherence through long-term planning
    \end{enumerate}
    \item Broad shift in state activities from ``market-steering'' to ``market-supporting,'' with specific focuses (in this volume) on regulation, welfare, and labor policies
\end{itemize}
